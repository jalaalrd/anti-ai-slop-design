# Copy: Words That Sell

A page can look designed and still read like a template. The words are half the design. This file is how to write and judge the words on a site or app: headlines, buttons, sections, empty states, error messages.

The three tests at the heart of it come from Harry Dry, who writes Marketing Examples. His site and his long interview with David Perell are the best places to go deeper:

What follows is our summary in our own words, with our own examples.

## The Three Tests

Run every headline, subheading and claim through all three. If a line fails one, rewrite it until it passes.

### 1. Can I picture it?

People remember what they can see in their heads. Abstract words leave nothing behind.

| Fails | Passes |
|---|---|
| "Fast, reliable delivery" | "Jollof at your door before the kettle boils" |
| "Smart scheduling for salons" | "Your Saturday bookings, filled while you sleep" |
| "Large storage capacity" | "Every receipt from the last five years, in your pocket" (only if it's true) |

Ask: could someone draw this line? If not, find the object, the place, the moment or the number that makes it real.

### 2. Could it be proven wrong?

A claim only means something if it could turn out to be false. "World-class service" can't be checked, so readers skip it. A specific fact can be checked, so they believe it.

| Fails | Passes |
|---|---|
| "Trusted by local bakeries" | "We deliver to 40 Leeds bakeries before 6am" (only if it's true) |
| "Best prices" | "₦1,500 delivery anywhere on the Island, no surge" (only if it's true) |
| "Super easy to use" | "Set up in 3 minutes, no app to download" (only if it's true) |

The numbers above show the form of a checkable claim. Never invent a number to pass this test; use only facts you have.

Only write facts that are true today. A fake number is worse than no number. If you don't have proof yet, say something smaller that is true ("Built with the owners of two Surulere salons", if you did) instead of something bigger that isn't.

### 3. Could only we say it?

If a competitor could put your headline on their site without changing a word, it isn't yours. Find the thing that's true of you and not of them: who you built it with, a detail of how it works, a choice you made that they didn't.

| Fails | Passes |
|---|---|
| "The all-in-one platform for small businesses" | "The booking sheet for salons tired of losing slots in WhatsApp" |
| "School payments made easy" | "Pay this term's fees from your phone and get the receipt on WhatsApp the same minute" (only if it's true) |

The headline must pass test 1. Tests 2 and 3 can be passed by the headline and subheading together: if the headline is a promise, the subheading must carry the checkable fact and the detail only you could claim.

A good check: swap your logo for a competitor's. If the words still fit, rewrite them.

## Rules for Every Line

These come from the same school of thinking, put in our own words.

- **Cut words.** Write the headline, then remove every word that isn't working. Shorter is almost always stronger.
- **Say what the reader gets, not what you can do.** "Get paid on time" beats "Automated invoice reminders".
- **Active voice.** "We send the reminder" beats "Reminders are sent".
- **Write the way you'd say it out loud.** Use "you" and "we". If you wouldn't say it to a customer's face, don't write it.
- **No landing-page buzzwords.** Seamless, robust, empower, leverage, revolutionise, all-in-one, next-gen: nobody talks like that, so nobody believes it. (anti-ai-slop-writing has the full list.)
- **Fewer adjectives and adverbs.** A specific noun or number does more than "amazing" or "incredibly".
- **Short sentences.** A full stop beats a comma most of the time.
- **Make the first line earn the second.** A headline that pulls the reader into the next line beats a clever one that ends the conversation.
- **Easy to scan.** Short paragraphs, clear subheadings, the important word early in each line.
- **A story beats a list.** One customer's before and after sticks better than five features.
- **Give the reader a reason to decide.** Show what not acting costs them, without fake urgency or countdown timers.
- **Copy is selling.** The goal is the action (sign up, book, pay), not a clever line.
- **Stay honest.** The truth told plainly reads warmer than hype.

## Where This Applies on a Page

| Element | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Headline | Passes all three tests. Uses the words your users used in interviews |
| Subheading | Says who it's for and what they get, in one sentence |
| Buttons | The action and the result: "Book my slot", "See today's menu", not "Submit" or "Get started" |
| How it works | Only if the content is a sequence the reader follows, such as the steps to book: then numbered steps a person could picture doing. Numbers used as decoration are a tell (slop.md). Otherwise a short story |
| Proof | One real quote with a real first name, or one checkable number |
| About / Team | Why you're building this, in plain words. Real names, real roles |
| Empty states | Tell the person what to do next: "No orders yet. Share your link to get your first one" |
| Errors | What went wrong and how to fix it, without blame: "That number looks short. Nigerian numbers have 11 digits" |
| Pricing | The real price, in the local currency, with what's included |

## How to Use This in the Workflow

1. **Collect the real words first.** Interview notes, WhatsApp messages from users, reviews of competitors. The best headline is often a sentence a user already said.
2. **Write the content before the layout** (SKILL.md, "Content First").
3. **Run the three tests** on every headline and claim. Rewrite until each line passes.
4. **Run the rules** above over the whole page.
5. **Read the page out loud.** Anything that sounds stiff, salesy or vague gets rewritten.
6. **Check every claim is true today.** Remove any number you can't back up.
7. If anti-ai-slop-writing is available, run the final copy through it as a last pass.

## Quick Check Before You Ship

- [ ] Could someone picture the headline?
- [ ] Could every claim on the page be proven wrong (so it means something)?
- [ ] Could a competitor paste our headline on their site? (It should be no.)
- [ ] Does every button say what happens next?
- [ ] Zero buzzwords, zero fake numbers, zero made-up testimonials?
- [ ] Does it read naturally out loud?
